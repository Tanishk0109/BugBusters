Module 3: Basic Sentence Formation

A guide for building basic sentences in sign language and communication through facial expressions.

1. Introduction to Sentences

Sentences are the foundation of communication. In this module, we will learn how to structure basic sentences using sign language and facial expressions.

Here, we see a visual representation of how sentences are structured in communication. Sentence formation is important for clear interaction, especially for Deaf and Mute individuals using sign language.

2. Combining Words to Form Sentences

By combining individual words in a logical order, you can form meaningful sentences. Example: "I love pizza" becomes a clear message.

This image shows how individual words can be combined to form a complete sentence. It's important to structure the words in the correct order for clarity.

  • Subject + Verb + Object: "I eat food"
  • Subject + Verb: "She runs"

3. Yes/No Questions

In sign language, yes/no questions can be formed by altering facial expressions. Example: A raised eyebrow with a shake of the head indicates a "no" answer.

This image demonstrates how a simple question is formed using facial expressions, with raised eyebrows to indicate a question and shaking or nodding of the head to indicate a response.

  • Example: "Do you like it?" – raised eyebrows and a questioning look.
  • Response: "Yes" – nodding head, "No" – shaking head.

4. WH Questions

WH questions include words like "who," "what," "where," "when," "why," and "how." They are used to gather information. The facial expression changes to show curiosity or the need for more information.

In this visual, we see a person asking a WH question, with a typical facial expression showing curiosity. The raised eyebrows and slight head tilt help indicate a question that seeks more detail.

  • Example: "Where are you going?" – raise eyebrows and lean slightly forward.
  • Response: Use clear sign language to answer with a statement or description.

5. Facial Expressions

Facial expressions play a significant role in communication for deaf and mute individuals. These expressions can indicate emotions, questions, and tone of the sentence.

This image highlights different facial expressions used to convey emotions or questions. Smiling, raising eyebrows, and furrowing your brow are important aspects of sign language and non-verbal communication.

  • Surprised: Raised eyebrows, open mouth.
  • Confused: Furrowed brows, slight tilt of the head.
  • Happy: Smiling with relaxed eyes.
